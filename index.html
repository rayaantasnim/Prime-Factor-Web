01. Strategy 01 - Parity & Modulo 5 Isolation

The initial mathematical pass executes immediate extraction of terminal factors through rapid digit inspection algorithms. Decadic zero stripping systematically strips factor pairs of (2 × 5)^k from any target integer ending in 0, dramatically reducing multi-digit numbers before deeper division passes. Concurrently, even composite stripping continually extracts powers of 2 from any integer terminating in the even set {0, 2, 4, 6, 8}. Terminal 5 extraction immediately isolates and removes all factorable 5s, collapsing complex composites down to odd integers ending exclusively in 1, 3, 7, or 9. By pruning these basic prime building blocks in sub-millisecond execution cycles, the candidate’s mental calculation search space is drastically condensed, leaving only high-value odd factor combinations for downstream algorithmic processing.

02. Strategy 02 - Digit Sum & Modulo 9 Heuristics

The second cognitive pipeline focuses on rapid identification of 3 and 3² = 9 divisors using digital root summation mechanics. By evaluating the congruences N ≡ S(N) (mod 3) and N ≡ S(N) (mod 9), the engine executes the classic "Casting Out Nines" shortcut in real-time mental calculation. This technique instantly identifies whether a composite target contains powers of 3, allowing candidates to strip out 3s and 9s without spending precious seconds running structural trial division. Eliminating these factors early prevents wasteful division steps against large primes and clears the structural path for high-speed identification of higher-order prime factors such as 7, 11, and 13.

03. Strategy 03 - Fermat's Difference of Squares Heuristic

Designed for advanced mental decomposition of odd composites, this strategy leverages the algebraic identity N = a² - b² = (a - b)(a + b), where a = ceil(sqrt(N)) + k. Candidates quickly evaluate perfect square boundaries near the target integer to expose twin prime factors or close prime pairs. In the benchmark case study of N = 899, computing ceil(sqrt(899)) gives 30. Evaluating 30² - 899 yields 900 - 899 = 1 = 1², deriving the factor pair (30 - 1)(30 + 1) = 29 × 31 in sub-200ms mental bursts. This heuristic completely eliminates traditional division grids for near-square composites, serving as a primary speed weapon in elite Olympiad arenas.
